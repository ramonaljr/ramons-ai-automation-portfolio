## Why most internal chatbots get abandoned

They get built, demoed well, and then quietly stop being used.

The reason is almost always the same: somebody asked it something important, got a confident answer, acted on it, and found out later it was invented. After that, nobody trusts it, and a tool nobody trusts is dead weight.

## Citations change the contract

When every answer carries a link back to the document and section it came from, the assistant stops being an oracle and becomes a very fast index. That is a much easier thing to trust, and a much more honest description of what retrieval actually does.

Practically, this means the generation step is only allowed to answer from retrieved chunks, and the response template requires a source for each claim.

## What I do when retrieval finds nothing

Return nothing.

"I could not find this in the indexed documents" is a correct, useful answer. A model asked to be helpful will happily fill that silence with something plausible, so the threshold has to be enforced in the pipeline rather than requested in the prompt.

## Keeping the index honest

Documents change. An index built once is wrong within a month.

The watcher on the source folder matters more than the model choice — when a document is edited, its chunks are re-embedded and the old ones removed. Otherwise you get the worst outcome available: a confident, cited answer pointing at a version that no longer exists.

## The part that is not about AI

Most of the work in a good knowledge base is deciding what goes in it. Indexing everything produces a system that retrieves plausible-looking noise.

Scope it to the documents that answer real, repeated questions. That is a librarian's job, not an engineer's, and it is where the value actually comes from.

## About this build

Built on Make for **Juniper & Moss, a fictional sample client** selling mugs, tote bags and candles online. The scenario itself is real and complete, and has been run end to end on sample orders that cover all three stock outcomes.

## The problem

- **Promises before checks.** Orders were confirmed without anyone looking at stock.
- **Surprise sell-outs.** Nobody noticed an item running low until it had run out.
- **Customers left guessing.** When an order could not be filled, the customer heard nothing.
- **No record.** There was no note of what happened to each order, or when.

## What I built

1. **Find new orders.** Every 15 minutes, the order sheet is searched for orders marked New, up to 10 per run, and each product is looked up by its SKU in the inventory sheet.
2. **Route by stock.** A Make Router compares the quantity ordered with the stock on hand and sends each order down every route that applies.
3. **Confirm the order.** When there is enough stock, the quantity is taken off the inventory, the order is marked Confirmed with a note of what was reserved and how much is left, and the customer gets a confirmation email.
4. **Flag low stock.** When the order leaves stock below the reorder level, operations gets a Slack alert naming the product, the stock left and the reorder level. The order is still confirmed.
5. **Hold short orders.** When there is not enough stock, the order is marked On hold with the quantity ordered and available, the customer is told about the delay, and operations is alerted in Slack to follow up within one business day.

## Safeguards

- **Plain rules, no guesswork:** every decision compares two numbers from the sheets.
- **Stock is reduced only for orders that can be filled in full,** so it never drops below zero.
- **Each order is stamped** with its outcome and the time it was handled, so the sheet shows the full history.
- **The delay email makes no promise of a date;** a person replies with a restock date or an alternative.

## Outcomes

- Every order is checked against stock before it is confirmed.
- Customers get a confirmation or an honest delay notice, never silence.
- Operations sees low stock early and every held order the moment it happens.
